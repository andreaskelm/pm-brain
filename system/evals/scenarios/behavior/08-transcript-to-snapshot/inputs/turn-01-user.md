Here's the transcript from my call with Jonas, a logistics lead at a mid-size distributor. Make me the interview snapshot for it.

Interviewer: Walk me through the last time a delivery went out late.

Jonas: Last month, a big retail order, about forty pallets. It left the warehouse three days after the date sales had given the customer. I found out when the customer called our service desk, not from our own system. I rang the warehouse supervisor, we pulled two people off another job, and it shipped that Thursday.

Interviewer: So the real issue is that sales promises dates the warehouse can't hit?

Jonas: Maybe. I wouldn't put it like that. I think it depends on the week.

Interviewer: What changed in how you plan since then?

Jonas: Honestly, not a lot yet. We talked about a weekly check between sales and the warehouse, but I don't think anyone has started it.

Interviewer: And what's coming next?

Jonas: Next quarter we'll move to a new routing tool, so this problem should mostly go away. That's the plan anyway.

Interviewer: Anything about who decides when an order is "ready"?

Jonas: Off the record, the director was never going to sign off on more warehouse staff, so we just live with it. Please don't put that in anything. On the record, the supervisor decides when pallets are staged, but the [inaudible] team marks it ready in the system, and those are not always the same moment.
