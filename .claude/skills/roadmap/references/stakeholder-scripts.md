# Stakeholder Scripts

Most roadmap fights are about dates, and most date requests hide a decision the person needs to make. Find that decision and you can usually answer it without promising anything. Adapt the wording; the move is the same each time.

## "When exactly will X be ready?"

> "X is part of [initiative], which is about [outcome]. It's in [Now/Next] at [High/Medium] confidence, so our honest range is [time range]. What are you planning around it? If there's a specific date that matters on your side, tell me and I'll tell you how realistic it is."

The question at the end does the work. "My customer's rollout starts in September" is answerable. "I just want a date" usually isn't a real need yet.

## "Why can't you just commit to a date?"

> "For things in Now, I can give you a tight range, and I will. Further out, any date I give you is a guess, and I'd rather you plan on a range that holds than a date that moves twice. What decision would a date help you make?"

If they need a real commitment (contract, regulation), take it seriously: get an eng-backed date, name what gets bumped, and mark it as committed on the roadmap. Don't let it hide among the confidence-labeled items.

## "The roadmap keeps changing!"

> "It changes when we learn something. Here's what moved since last time and why. Is [outcome] still the thing that matters most to you? If yes, we're still pointed the right way, even if the route changed."

This only works if you keep the "what changed since last version" list. Without it, every change looks like a miss.

## "What if [external factor] happens?"

> "That's already priced into the confidence on [initiative]: it's [level] partly because of [factor]. If it lands, here's what we'd shift." (If one factor would flip the whole plan, show a scenario version instead.)

## Cutting it for each audience

- **Leadership** — outcomes and themes, the confidence behind them, and the dependencies that need their help to unblock. Lead with what you're NOT doing and why.
- **Engineering** — technical context, the dependency map, and an open invitation to challenge scope and timing. They should have shaped the Now column, not received it.
- **Sales, CS, business teams** — how each item connects to what their customers feel, what's explicitly not coming, and the confidence behind any range they might repeat to a customer. Assume anything you show them will be forwarded.
- **Compliance and legal** — compliance-related outcomes, regulatory timing with caveats, and how risk is being reduced.
