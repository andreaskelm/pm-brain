# Meeting Agendas

An agenda is a written promise about what the meeting will produce. If you can't write the outcome in one line ("Decide whether we ship EU without SSO"), you probably don't need a meeting yet, you need a doc or a conversation.

## Should this even be a meeting?

- Could it be async? Status, FYI and "just so everyone knows" can almost always be written.
- What decision or outcome do you need, and who decides? Consensus, majority, or one named decider? Say it before the meeting, not during.
- Who MUST be there, meaning you can't proceed without them? Everyone else gets the notes.
- If you had 15 minutes, how would you run it? Book that, not the default hour.

## Decision-first agenda (the default for stakeholder meetings)

```markdown
**[Meeting]: [the outcome, e.g. "Decide EU launch scope"]**
**Date / length:** [ ] · **Decider:** [name] · **Decision rule:** [decider decides after input / consensus]
**Required:** [names] · **Optional / notes only:** [names]

**Pre-read (sent 24h before):** [one-pager link]. We'll assume you've read it.

1. **Context check** (5 min) — any questions on the pre-read? No re-presenting it.
2. **Options and trade-offs** (15 min) — [option A], [option B]; what each costs.
3. **Decision** (10 min) — decider calls it, or names what's missing and by when.
4. **Actions and comms** (5 min) — owners, dates, who tells whom.

**Parking lot:** [off-topic items, with an owner to follow up]
```

End every topic with an explicit decision or an explicit next step with an owner. Notes go out within 24 hours: decisions, actions with owners and dates, and what was parked. Absent stakeholders get them too.

## Sprint review (with stakeholders)

```markdown
**Sprint [#] review: [sprint goal in one sentence]**
Prep: demos tested, stakeholders invited, feedback capture ready

1. Goal reminder and result (5 min) — achieved / partly / not, in one line each
2. Demos (most of the time) — per item: why we built it (2 min), demo (5–10), feedback (5)
3. Feedback and priority impact (15 min) — what worked, what didn't, does anything change in the backlog?
4. Look ahead (5 min) — next sprint focus, input needed from stakeholders
Capture: feedback, new backlog items, priority changes, follow-ups
```

Velocity numbers mean little to stakeholders. Lead with the goal and the outcomes; keep metrics for the team.

## Strategy or planning session

```markdown
**[Quarter] planning: [the decision, e.g. "Pick 3 priorities for Q3"]**
Pre-read 48h before: current metrics, strategic options, stakeholder input

1. Context (15 min) — where we are: metrics, wins, what's not working
2. What's changed (30 min) — which assumptions no longer hold, new opportunities
3. Options (60 min) — present, pressure-test against agreed criteria
4. Decide (45 min) — priorities, success metrics, owners, rough resourcing
5. Comms (15 min) — who tells whom, by when, and what we say we're NOT doing
```

Don't run it if the homework isn't done. A planning session without pre-reads turns into the pre-read, live, at five times the cost.

## Antipatterns

- **Meeting for meeting's sake** — "we've always had this". Cancel any recurring meeting with no agenda 24h before; challenge each one quarterly.
- **Status meeting** — everyone reports, nobody decides. Move status to writing; keep the slot for discussion.
- **Never-ending meeting** — no timeboxes. Put minutes on items, show a timer, end five minutes early.
- **No decision made** — same topic three meetings running. Name the decider and the decision rule upfront.
- **Wrong people** — decider absent, ten FYI attendees. Reschedule rather than meet without the decider.

Team ceremonies (standup, planning, refinement, retro) and 1:1s are team process, not stakeholder communication, so they're not covered here.
